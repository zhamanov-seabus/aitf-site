Mission & Vision

Why we exist

Mission

The NAU AI Task Force advances the responsible, effective, and ethical use of artificial intelligence across teaching, learning, research, scholarship, and operations. In service of the University’s mission of teaching excellence and student-centeredness, it explores emerging AI capabilities, pilots and implements practical solutions, strengthens the University’s research and scholarly capacity, and advises leadership on the strategic adoption of AI — safeguarding academic integrity, human dignity, and the University’s values.

Vision

To make North American University a recognized model for the responsible integration of AI in higher education — where AI amplifies teaching excellence, accelerates faculty and student research and scholarship, expands student success and global competency, strengthens institutional effectiveness, and is governed by clear ethical standards that keep people, not technology, at the center.

Strategic Alignment

University goals → Task Force pillars

The strategy is organized as five pillars, each a direct instrument of one of NAU’s five strategic goals (2025–2028). The Task Force advances the University’s plan — it does not run parallel to it.

Goal 1 · Enhance the quality of academic programs1 · AI for Academic Quality & Research — curriculum & faculty AI literacy; research productivity, tools & integrity
Goal 2 · Strengthen institutional effectiveness2 · AI for Institutional Effectiveness — AI in operations, training, data-informed decisions
Goal 3 · Student-centeredness & global citizenship3 · AI for Students & Global Citizenship — advising, tutoring, accessibility, multilingual support
Goal 4 · Engage with stakeholders4 · Governance, Ethics & Engagement — policies, standards, transparent stakeholder input
Goal 5 · Increase financial capacity & resources5 · AI Capacity & Resources — grants, partnerships, infrastructure & tools
Strategy in Detail

The five pillars

1
NAU Goal 1

AI for Academic Quality & Research

  • Integrate AI into curriculum, including AI / agentic-AI concentrations
  • Build faculty AI literacy and teaching innovation
  • Enable AI-assisted research: literature review, analysis, coding, scholarly writing; grow research output
  • Uphold academic integrity, building on the Student AI Usage Policy
2
NAU Goal 2

AI for Institutional Effectiveness

  • Pilot AI in operations: admissions, advising, registration, help desk, communications
  • Deliver a campus-wide AI training program
  • Support data-informed, timely decision-making across departments
3
NAU Goal 3

AI for Students & Global Citizenship

  • AI-powered support: advising agents, tutoring, accessibility, multilingual help
  • Keep student needs and career goals central
  • Teach responsible AI citizenship
4
NAU Goal 4

Governance, Ethics & Engagement

  • Establish AI governance: policies, guidelines, responsible-use standards
  • Engage faculty, students, employers, and community
  • Communicate transparently; advise leadership
5
NAU Goal 5

AI Capacity & Resources

  • Pursue grants, partnerships, and sponsorships (including research funding)
  • Invest cost-effectively in AI infrastructure and tools
  • Build sustainable AI capacity

Operational Plan · AY 2026–2027

From onboarding to action

Before advising the University, the committee reaches a shared baseline through four onboarding sessions, then executes a three-phase action plan across the year.

Fall 2026
Foundation
  • Onboard & train the Task Force
  • Adopt charter, mission, vision & strategy
  • AI inventory & needs assessment
  • Draft responsible-use guidelines
  • University-wide AI literacy training
  • Launch this website
  • Shortlist 2–3 pilots & secure funding
Spring 2027
Pilots & Enablement
  • Run pilots (advising / help-desk agent; faculty research toolkit; one operations automation)
  • Adopt academic-integrity & research-conduct standards for AI
  • Recommend a governance framework (use, data, privacy)
Summer 2027
Evaluate & Scale
  • Evaluate pilots against metrics; decide what to scale
  • Deliver a phased AI roadmap & funding plan
  • Year-end report; set 2027–2028 priorities

All year, cross-cutting: stakeholder engagement (faculty, students, employers); quarterly updates to leadership; a research emphasis, including pursuit of at least one external grant.

Pilot Resources

What the pilots need

To run the Fall 2026–Spring 2027 pilots responsibly, the Task Force requests controlled institutional support, tied to defined pilots and success metrics.

Compute / hostingA dedicated server or cloud VM for the advising agent, RAG knowledge base, and usage logging.
LLM API accessMetered API usage that powers the pilots, primarily the 24/7 advising assistant.
Tool subscriptionsTeam licenses for the AI tools used in the teaching and operations pilots.

Support is requested as a controlled, metrics-tied allocation and reviewed against pilot outcomes. Detailed resourcing is provided to leadership in the Operational Plan.
